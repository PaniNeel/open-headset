# Open Headset

**Keep the sound. Rebuild the structure.**

An open-source, 3D-printable headphone platform designed to give working components from broken headsets a second life.

---

> # 🚧 WORK IN PROGRESS
>
> Open Headset is currently in early development. The concept, mechanical architecture, compatibility and acoustic performance are not yet validated.

---

## The Idea

What if you could print a new pair of headphones using the components from your broken ones?

Headphones often become unusable because their plastic structure breaks, even when the speakers and cables still work.

Instead of throwing everything away, Open Headset aims to let you recover those components and install them in a new, 3D-printed structure.

The goal is to develop a modular headphone platform that anyone can repair, customize, manufacture and improve.

## How It Works

1. **Recover** working speakers, cables and other reusable components.
2. **Measure** their dimensions and mounting requirements.
3. **Adapt** the components to the Open Headset platform.
4. **Print** the required parts at home.
5. **Assemble** your headset and test the result.
6. **Share** your improvements with the community.

The initial development will focus on validating the design with real components before expanding compatibility.

## One Common Base

Open Headset is built around a shared design foundation.

The project aims to maintain **one official core**: a common mechanical architecture that accepted contributions can reuse and extend.

This avoids creating a disconnected collection of incompatible designs while allowing the platform to evolve.

### The Official Core

The official core is the reference design maintained by the project.

Community contributions can improve it, provided they follow the project's design principles and are reviewed before being accepted.

Examples include:

- Compatible driver adapters.
- Improved earcup designs.
- Alternative enclosure geometries.
- Better mechanical connections.
- Validated structural improvements.
- Documented acoustic experiments.

Not every contribution needs to be merged into the core. Some improvements may work better as separate, compatible modules.

### Community Designs

Open Headset also encourages independent experimentation.

Anyone can create alternative headphone designs, explore new mechanisms or completely rethink the structure.

These designs do not have to follow the official architecture and do not need to be integrated into the main project.

They should simply be documented honestly, including their compatibility and testing status.

### Three Design Statuses

| Status               | Meaning                                                                                                         |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Official**         | Reviewed and accepted into the main project.                                                                    |
| **Community Module** | A separate component designed for the official platform, with its integration and validation status documented. |
| **Experimental**     | An independent or unvalidated design that may differ from the official architecture.                            |

The distinction is about compatibility, review and project status — not creative freedom.

**One common foundation. One evolving official design. Unlimited room to experiment.**

## Why 3D Printing?

### ♻️ Repair Instead of Replace

Reuse functional components and replace only the structure that is broken.

### 🧩 Modular Design

Develop interchangeable interfaces and components instead of redesigning the entire headset for every variation.

### 🏠 Local Manufacturing

Print replacement parts at home or through a local makerspace, reducing dependence on proprietary replacement parts.

### 🎨 Customization

Explore different shapes, dimensions, materials and ergonomic configurations.

### 🔬 Acoustic Experimentation

A headphone enclosure is part of an acoustic system. Its geometry, internal volume, sealing, wall thickness and structural behaviour can influence the resulting sound.

3D printing makes it possible to investigate these variables through controlled design changes.

Potential areas of research include:

- Infill patterns and density.
- Internal enclosure volume and geometry.
- Wall thickness and structural rigidity.
- Printed lattice structures and vibration transmission.
- Driver mounting and sealing.
- Earpad geometry and driver positioning.

These are experimental variables, not guaranteed sound improvements. Their effects must be evaluated through measurements and listening tests.

The long-term goal is to make headphone design more accessible, measurable and open to experimentation.

## Contributing

Open Headset is intended to grow through community participation.

Contributions may include:

- Parametric CAD models and mechanical design.
- Measurements from donor headphones.
- Compatible adapters and modular components.
- Print settings and material comparisons.
- Assembly instructions and build documentation.
- Acoustic measurements and reproducible tests.
- Independent experimental designs.

Contributors should clearly distinguish measured results from assumptions and untested concepts.

The official contribution and review guidelines will be developed as the project matures.

## Current Status

**Phase: THE IDEA — Early Development**

Open Headset is not yet a finished headphone design.

The initial priority is to build and test a functional prototype using salvaged components from an existing headset.

Universal compatibility, mechanical reliability, comfort and acoustic performance remain unverified.

The project is being shared early to establish its direction and invite collaboration.

## Long-Term Vision

A broken headband or earcup should not automatically turn working electronics into waste.

Open Headset aims to provide an open platform where people can reuse what they already own, manufacture a new structure and contribute to a design that everyone can build upon.

**Less waste. More repair. Open hardware. Better ways to experiment.**
